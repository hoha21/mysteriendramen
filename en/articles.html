  • 31 October 2026 · Contexts

    A mystery drama for today? Where reincarnation and karma surface in life

    Prompted by Andreas Laudert’s project for a contemporary mystery drama and his reading in Basel on 30 October, the article asks where questions of reincarnation and karma arise without spiritual training and without relying on what others report: in encounters, in recurring conflicts, in responsibility, and in looking back on one’s own life. With Lessing’s remark that forgetting earlier lives is a blessing, it suggests that a drama for today would let karma emerge as a question in conversation rather than explain it. (Article in German)

  • 30 October 2026 · Contexts

    Redemption as a path? The Mystery Dramas and messianism

    The years around 1900 were full of messianic expectation. The article distinguishes Steiner’s talk of the reappearance of Christ in the etheric — a change in human perception, not the arrival of a person — from the messianism of his time, looks at Theodora’s vision in The Portal of Initiation, and sets the plays beside the conflict over Krishnamurti, in the course of which the Anthroposophical Society was founded. (Article in German)

  • 29 October 2026 · Contexts

    Steiner among his contemporaries — Schuré, Maeterlinck, Hofmannsthal

    The Mystery Dramas as theatre of literary modernism: Édouard Schuré as a forerunner, Maurice Maeterlinck and Hugo von Hofmannsthal as neighbours and counterpoles. The plays ask the same questions as their contemporaries — how theatre can show the inner life, what language can still achieve — and answer them in their own way, with a drama of conversation that makes knowing itself the dramatic event. (Article in German)

    Johannes Thomasius — portrait of a developing figure

    Johannes Thomasius is the central developing figure of the four plays: from the painter who follows Maria, through the retrospect into an earlier life, to the self-knowledge before the Guardian of the Threshold and the meeting with his Double. (Article in German)

    Mirror metaphors in the Mystery Dramas

    Why the plays again and again let their characters meet themselves — as a Double, as an earlier life, as a higher self. Where Romantic literature made the encounter with one’s double an image of terror, the Mystery Dramas make it the condition of knowledge. Based on my doctoral thesis (Basel 2005/2006).
