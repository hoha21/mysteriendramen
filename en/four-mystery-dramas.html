The Four Mystery Dramas: An Overview

Between 1910 and 1913 Rudolf Steiner wrote four closely connected plays that are usually called the “Mystery Dramas” today. Steiner gave each play a subtitle of its own; The Portal of Initiation, for instance, he called “A Rosicrucian Mystery”. Each was first performed in Munich in August of its year — a small festival rhythm of its own, spread over four summers.

The Portal of Initiation (1910)

Die Pforte der Einweihung. First performed on 15 August 1910 at the Schauspielhaus in Munich. At its centre is the painter Johannes Thomasius, who sets out on a path of initiation together with Maria under the guidance of the spiritual teacher Benedictus. Johannes has to come to know himself in painful inner trials, and in doing so he encounters the adversary powers Lucifer and Ahriman. Around him stand the learned Professor Capesius and the scientifically trained, sceptical Doctor Strader — two figures who, each in his own way, are also searching for knowledge.

The Soul’s Probation (1911)

Die Prüfung der Seele. First performed on 17 August 1911 at the Gärtnerplatz-Theater in Munich. The second play continues the action but turns the view towards the past: Capesius, Maria and Johannes experience a retrospect into earlier incarnations in the fourteenth century. There Capesius is the First Preceptor of the Templar order and the head of a mine; he had separated from his family early on, before he became a Templar. The individuality we know as Maria was then a monk, preacher and administrator of the church, who upheld the cause of the church against the Templar order. Johannes, in this constellation, is the son of the earlier Capesius and at the same time a pupil of that monk. It is the monk who estranges Johannes from his father, because the two hold different views of truth and spirit. This entanglement has to be transformed in the present through conscious knowledge.

The Guardian of the Threshold (1912)

Der Hüter der Schwelle. First performed on 24 August 1912, again at the Gärtnerplatz-Theater in Munich. Johannes falls under the influence of Lucifer, and his love turns to Theodora. The “Guardian of the Threshold” of the title is a stern watcher who demands that Johannes free himself from what binds him to the conditions of everyday life before he crosses into the spiritual world. Before him Johannes recognises that his being in love was not directed at Theodora but at a projection: he had fallen in love with an image of his own wishes that had merely taken on Theodora’s form. At this moment his Double steps towards him: „Erkenne mich – und schaue dich in mir.“ (“Know me – and see yourself in me.”)

The Souls’ Awakening (1913)

Der Seelen Erwachen. First performed on 22 August 1913 at the Volkstheater in Munich. The fourth and last play shows how the pupils around Benedictus try to put their spiritual insights into concrete, earthly work — an undertaking that threatens to fail through inner resistance and outer scepticism. At the end Strader dies; in his last lines he writes that the obstacles did not come only from outside but also from inner shortcomings of his work. At the same time, deeper karmic connections across the group’s earlier lives come to light.

What connects the four plays

All four plays share the same circle of characters — Johannes Thomasius, Maria, Capesius, Strader, Benedictus and others — and follow them across several incarnations. This “drama of reincarnation”, as one might call it, is the real dramaturgical construction: not a continuous plot in the classical sense, but a path of development that stretches across lifetimes and ages and is seen from a different angle in each of the four parts.
